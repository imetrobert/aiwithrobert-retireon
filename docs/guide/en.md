# Welcome: what the planner does
eyebrow: WELCOME
This planner answers one question: "Will my savings last for my whole retirement, and what should I take out of which account each year?"

You type in a few numbers (your age, your savings, your pensions and how much you want to spend). The planner then looks forward year by year, applies Ontario and federal tax rules, and shows you a plan. You do not need to know any formulas.

## What it does
- Shows if your money lasts to the age you choose, and tells you each year which account to take money from.
- Estimates income tax (federal, Ontario, surtax and Health Premium), tax held back at source, CPP and OAS.
- Lets you try "what if" scenarios and save them. Works for one person or for a couple.

## What it does not do
- It is not financial, tax or legal advice, and it does not connect to your bank, the CRA.
- It does not know the future: returns and inflation are assumptions you choose.

> **Signing in**
> Your access is given by Robert. Open the planner, sign in with your email and the code or password you chose, and a "Welcome" banner confirms who is signed in. The planner screens themselves never send your figures anywhere.

> **Good to know**
> The numbers you see when you first open the planner (age 55, $4,000 a month and so on) are examples. Replace them with your own. You can change anything at any time, and nothing is final until you decide.

## How the planner is organized
Three short steps collect your information, then a fourth screen shows your results. The row at the top shows where you are: **1 You, 2 Money, 3 Returns, 4 Results**. Use the blue **Next** and **Back** buttons at the bottom. The **Menu** button opens extra tools.

# Before you start: what to gather
eyebrow: PREPARATION
Ten minutes of preparation makes the planner much more accurate. Round numbers are fine. The same checklist is inside the planner: at the top of Step 1, tap **Before you start: what to gather (about 10 minutes)**.

| Find this | Where to look |
| Balances of your RRSP and TFSA | Your bank, credit union or investment firm statement or app |
| Balance of any locked-in account (LIRA, or LIF if you already have one) | The firm that holds it. Note whether it is regulated in Ontario (FSRA) or federally |
| Your employer pension: a monthly amount for life (defined benefit) or an account balance (defined contribution) | Your yearly pension statement, or your HR portal |
| Your CPP estimate at different ages | My Service Canada Account (your statement of contributions) |
| Your OAS estimate | My Service Canada Account, or canada.ca |
| Savings that are not in a registered account | Your bank or investment statements |
| What you want to spend each month after tax | Your last 12 months of bank and card statements, divided by 12 |
| Big one-time costs: a trip, a new roof, helping a child | Your own list. You will add these as life events |

> **Tip**
> No pension? Leave those cards at zero. Not sure about a number? Use your best guess and come back later. The planner saves your work on this device as you go.

> **Privacy**
> You never need to enter your name, Social Insurance Number or account numbers. Rough figures are enough.

# Money words explained
eyebrow: GLOSSARY
Retirement planning uses many acronyms. Here is each one in plain language.

| Term | In plain words |
| **RRSP** | A savings account where you got a tax deduction when you put money in. You pay tax when you take money out. |
| **RRIF** | What an RRSP becomes by the end of the year you turn 71. You must take out a minimum every year, and it is taxed. |
| **TFSA** | Growth and withdrawals are tax-free. The planner touches it last. |
| **LIRA / LIF** | "Locked-in" money, usually from an employer pension you left. A LIRA grows until you start income; it then becomes a LIF with a yearly minimum and maximum. |
| **Unlocking (50%)** | When you open a LIF in Ontario you may move up to half of the amount going into it, within 60 days, to an RRSP or RRIF, where there is no maximum. Federally regulated accounts have a similar one-time transfer. The planner can model this once, in the year it happens. |
| **DB pension** | Your employer promises a fixed monthly amount for life, based on pay and years of service. |
| **DC pension** | An account built from your contributions and your employer's. What you get depends on how it grows. |
| **CPP** | Canada Pension Plan. You choose when to start (60 to 70). Later means more per month. |
| **OAS** | Old Age Security, from age 65 (can be delayed to 70). High-income retirees may have part taken back (the "clawback"). |
| **Non-registered** | Savings outside RRSP and TFSA. Only the gain is taxed when you sell. |
| **Surtax** | An extra Ontario tax added when your Ontario tax is above certain amounts. It can make tax in retirement higher than expected. |
| **Health Premium** | An Ontario charge that rises with income, up to $900. |
| **Withholding tax** | Tax the institution holds back when you withdraw from a registered account. It is a down payment on your tax, not an extra tax. |
| **Today's / future dollars** | Today's dollars show what the money buys in value you understand now. Future dollars show the actual cheque amounts. |

# Step 1 · About you

side: step1.png, aishortcut.png
This screen asks who the plan is for and when you want to retire.

1. **Before you start** (closed list). The checklist from the previous chapter.
2. **Name this plan.** Optional, for example "Base case" or "Retire at 60". Helpful when you save several versions.
3. **Are you filling this out for you, or for you and a spouse?** "Just me", or "Me and my spouse". The couple option is explained in its own chapter. Choose it only if your spouse is willing to share their financial details with you, because you will both see each other's figures.
4. **Your age today and the age you want to retire.** Tick **I am already retired** if you have stopped working.
5. **Will you have other income during retirement?** Choose **No** (the default) or **Yes** if you expect part-time work, rental, business or similar income. Only **Yes** adds a card in Step 2. Do not count CPP, OAS or an employer pension here: they have their own cards.


Near the bottom of Step 1 are two optional shortcuts, both closed until you open them:

- **Read your CPP contributions statement.** Pick the PDF of your Service Canada statement of contributions. The planner reads it on your device (nothing is uploaded) and shows a summary of the years it found. It fills in your CPP amount only when the statement itself prints an estimate at age 65, and only after you tap **Apply**. A contribution history alone is never turned into a pension.
- **Optional shortcut: fill this in with an AI chat** (about 5 to 15 minutes). Copy the prompt, paste it into your own AI assistant, answer its questions, paste its code block back, then **Preview** and **Apply**. Hide your name, SIN and account numbers before attaching statements.

# Step 2 · Your money
eyebrow: STEP 2 OF 3
side: step2.png
Tap a card to open it. **Expand all** and **Collapse all** open or close every card.

1. **How often you are paid, and your spending.** Enter what you want to spend after tax, in today's dollars. The planner raises it with inflation each year. Choose per week, month or year.
2. **Your expenses (optional list).** Under the spending box, a link opens a list where you add each regular expense. See the chapter on the expense list.
3. **Inflation.** Leave the defaults unless you know why you would change them (about 2% a year).
4. **RRSP and TFSA.** Today's balance and how much you add each year until you retire.
5. **Locked-in account (LIRA / LIF).** Balance, yearly additions, and who regulates it: Ontario (most employers) or Federal (banks, telecoms, airlines, railways). Ontario LIFs use the Ontario maximum table; federal LIFs use the federal formula. Enter your own yearly cap only if your statement shows one. Tick **Unlock half of my locked-in money** and choose an age (55 or later) to model the one-time 50% transfer to an RRSP/RRIF.
6. **Your employer pension.** Fixed benefit for life (DB), Growing savings account (DC) or Both.
7. **Non-registered savings.** Fill in if you have them.
8. **Other income during retirement.** Only shown if you answered Yes in Step 1. See the chapter on other income.

> **Tip**
> Every card has a blue **Why this matters** button. Tap it any time you are unsure what a question means.

> **Locked-in rules**
> Check on your statement who regulates the locked-in account, Ontario or Federal. Choosing the wrong type changes your plan, so confirm with your plan administrator. A pension from another province can have different rules; treat the result as a rough guide.

Instead of typing, open **Read your CPP contributions statement** in Step 1 and choose your PDF from My Service Canada Account. The planner reads the contribution table and, when the statement prints a pension estimate, shows it so you can tick the one to use and tap **Apply**. Only the age-65 amount is applied.

> **If the planner cannot read your file**
> A scanned or photographed statement has no text to read. Download the PDF again from your online account. If the layout is not recognised, you can tap the right number yourself from the list the planner shows.

# Step 3 · Growth
eyebrow: STEP 3 OF 3
side: step3.png
The last input screen is about how fast your money grows and how long it must last.

1. **Yearly return until you retire (%).** The average growth you expect while you are still saving. Example: 6.0.
2. **How should returns work after you retire?** "Same return every year" is the simplest. Other choices test a bad market at the start of retirement or use your own measured history.
3. **Yearly return after you retire (%).** Usually lower, because retirees hold safer investments. Example: 4.5.
4. **Plan until age.** An age you do not want to outlive. Many people plan to 90 or 95.

> **Be realistic**
> Higher returns look better but are less certain. Try a lower number as well and compare both scenarios.

Tap **Show my plan**.

# Step 4 · Your results
eyebrow: RESULTS
side: results.png, mcrun.png
The top banner gives the headline: "You are on track. Your money lasts to age 90" (or a warning if it runs out earlier), with four key numbers:

- Total income tax over your retirement.
- OAS repaid (the clawback). "None" is good news.
- Savings left at the last age.
- Left to family after tax (the estate value).

Under it:

- **Your savings over time.** A chart of all accounts together.
- **What to do each year.** The year-by-year plan. Switch between the easy cards and the detailed table.

At the bottom, **Edit inputs** returns you to the steps and **Test different returns** (at the top of the results) takes you to the Growth step, where you can pick another set of return assumptions and press **Show my plan** to see how it changes your plan. The small green or red percentage under each age shows that year's market gain or loss.

## If you chose Monte Carlo (random markets)

Under the heading you will see a **This run** box. The headline numbers on the page come from ONE random run of the market, and the box says what kind of market it was:

- A verdict: a rougher-than-typical, typical or luckier-than-typical market.
- The average yearly return of this run next to the one you assumed, plus the worst and best year (with your age) and the average of the first five years.
- Where this run ended among the 300 simulated markets. A weak start hurts most, because you draw money while prices are down.

The "% of 300 simulated markets" line is the real odds. Tap **Re-run another scenario** to draw a new random set of markets and recalculate; the scenario number goes up each time. **Show my plan** starts again at scenario 1. If "same random markets every run" is ticked, scenario 1 repeats every time, while each re-run gives a different one.

Under the big banner there is a **Judge the plan by** switch. **This run** (the default) decides "You are on track" from the single run shown. **All markets** decides it from the share of the 300 simulated markets that fund the plan: 90% or more is "You are on track", 70% to 89% is "Some risk", and under 70% is "Needs a change". The tax and savings numbers always come from the one run. The choice stays when you tap **Re-run another scenario**.

# Compare and stress-test your plan
eyebrow: STEP 4
At the bottom of your results, open **Compare and stress-test**. Each button re-runs your plan with one thing changed and shows the results side by side. Nothing in your plan is changed. The first row is always your plan as entered, and the highlighted row is the one that leaves the most to your family after tax while still paying for your spending.

- **When to start CPP and OAS.** Compares starting CPP at 60, 65 or 70 and OAS at 65 or 70.
- **Compare withdrawal strategies.** Compares spending the TFSA or non-registered savings first, taking extra RRSP/RRIF money in the early years, and topping up the TFSA.
- **Test weak markets early on.** Shows whether your money still lasts if the first retirement years are poor.
- **Estate view.** Shows what your family receives, the tax at death, and the same plan if it ended earlier or later.

To compare two saved plans, open **Save / load scenarios** from the Menu and open **Compare two scenarios**. To share your plan, open **Save or share** and tap **One-page summary**: your phone's print sheet opens, where you choose "Save to Files". In the Menu, **Text size** makes everything larger.

# How to read a yearly card
eyebrow: READING THE PLAN
side: yearcard.png
Tap any year in **What to do each year**. It opens into a **Do this** list, in the order you would act.

1. **Total to take out this year** (dark blue bar): spending + taxes + anything moved to another account.
2. **One block per account**, for example RRSP/RRIF or Non-registered. Each shows what to withdraw and what that money pays for: living costs, tax, and **Goes to life events**, which lists each event by the name you gave it on the Life events screen (recurring or one-time) with the amount that account covers. When several accounts pay for events in the same year, each account's amount is shared across the events in proportion to their cost.

   When part of an account's withdrawal is moved into another account (for example into the TFSA), the source block shows a blue **Moved into …** line and the receiving block shows a green **Transfer in from …** line, so you can see where the money came from.
3. **The yellow note** says who handles the tax. "Bank holds back $X at source, so you receive $Y in pocket" means the institution withholds a down payment on your tax. "There is no withholding tax, so put this money aside" means you must keep money for the tax bill.
4. **Left after growth.** Start of year, minus withdrawn, plus market growth, equals what is left. The leftover is often higher than start minus withdrawal because your investments grew.
5. **Not touched this year** lists accounts the plan leaves alone, usually your TFSA.
6. **How each dollar is used** splits the money into spending, taxes and moved or reinvested. The green line checks that money in equals money out.

> **Unlocking year**
> In the year part of a locked-in account becomes an unlocked RRSP/RRIF, a banner shows the calculation: the percentage unlocked, which account it came from, and the old and new balances.

> **Who withholds tax at source?**
> Yes: employer pension payments, LIRA/LIF, RRSP/RRIF and unlocked RRSP/RRIF, on the part above the yearly minimum (an RRSP before 71 and a brand-new unlocked account withhold everything). No: non-registered savings and TFSA. Tax withheld is credited when you file your return.

# Other income in retirement
eyebrow: STEP 2 TOOL
Some people expect income besides pensions and savings: part-time work, rent from a property, a small business. This is optional. If you answered **Yes** to "Will you have other income during retirement?" in Step 1, a card **Other income during retirement** appears in Step 2. If you answered No, nothing changes.

- **One amount for every year.** Type the income you expect each year, before tax. It is used for every year of your retirement.
- **A different amount each year.** Tap **Optional: enter a different amount for each year**. The planner builds the list for you from your age today, the age you retire (Step 1) and the age you plan until (Step 3): one row per year, with your age and the calendar year already filled in. You only type the income expected that year. Leave a year blank if you expect nothing. **Fill every year** copies one amount into every row.
- Tap **Save for this scenario**. Your single yearly box turns grey. To change the list tap **Edit the list**, or **Stop using the list** to type one amount again.
- **Which dollars?** Choose on the card. **Today's dollars (plus inflation)** raises the amounts for inflation each year, like your spending. **Dollars of each year (no inflation)** uses the numbers exactly as you typed them.
- If you change your age or retirement age later, the list follows. The amounts you typed stay with the same ages.

> **How it is taxed**
> The planner treats this money as ordinary taxable income (like work, rental or business income). It lowers the withdrawals you need from your savings, and it can raise your tax and the OAS clawback. It is not treated as pension income. Leave out money that is tax-free.

> **Couples**
> In "Me and my spouse" mode each of you answers for yourself, in your own form.

# Selling your home
eyebrow: OPTIONAL
Step 1 asks whether you plan to **sell your home during retirement**. Choose **Yes, and move to a smaller home** or **Yes, and rent instead** and a card called "Selling your home" appears in Step 2.

1. **Age when you sell.** Never earlier than your retirement age.
2. **Expected sale price, in today's dollars.** The planner raises it with inflation.
3. **Selling costs.** Agent, legal and notary fees. About 5% is common.
4. **Mortgage or loans paid off at the sale.**
5. **Smaller home only:** its price in today's dollars and the buying and moving costs.
6. **Yearly housing costs you stop paying**, and **the yearly cost of your new housing** (rent, or tax and upkeep of the smaller home). Your yearly spending changes by the difference from the sale year on.
7. **Where the money goes.** "TFSA first" puts as much as your unused TFSA room allows into the TFSA and the rest into your non-registered account. "All in my non-registered account" skips the TFSA.

The money from selling your main home is not taxed, so the whole amount left after costs, your mortgage and the smaller home is invested in the year you sell. The card shows that amount in today's dollars before you calculate. The results then say how much went into each account, in the plan's summary and on that year's card.

> **Good to know**
> A TFSA grows tax-free but only takes what your room allows. Money in a non-registered account is taxed every year on its income and gains. As a couple, answer on one person's form only, so the sale is not counted twice. The planner does not model a new mortgage, a second property or a reverse mortgage.

# The expense list
eyebrow: STEP 2 TOOL
side: expenses_top.png, expenses_rows.png
Under the spending question in Step 2, tap the link to open **Your expenses** (it is also in the Menu). Use it when it is easier to add up bills than to guess one monthly figure.

- Add one row per **recurring** expense: a name, an amount (shown with a $), and how often you are billed: **Monthly, Weekly or Yearly**. Enter the amount as you are billed. **Set all rows to** changes every row at once.

## Sharing expenses in a couple
In couple mode each row has two linked boxes, one for each of you. Type one percentage and the other adjusts so they always add up to 100%. Leave both blank to use the automatic split. **Apply to all rows** sets every row at once.

# Me and my spouse
eyebrow: COUPLES
side: couple.png, household.png
Choose **Me and my spouse** at the top of Step 1. You fill in your own form, then switch with **Filling in** to fill in your spouse's. Add first names (optional) to make the tabs clear. Your spouse must agree to share their financial details, because both forms are visible in the same plan.

- **One household spending target.** Spending, inflation and returns are entered once and shared.
- **How the spending is split.** Automatically by income, or by the percentages you type in the expense list (each row has linked You and Spouse boxes that always add up to 100%). **Recommend an optimized split** suggests the split that works best for the household.
- **Results.** A **Household** tab shows both people together, plus a tab for each person. Each person is planned with the normal single-person engine on their share.
- **Pension income splitting** (on by default): each year up to 50% of the higher earner's eligible pension income is moved to the other spouse for tax and OAS clawback, only when it lowers combined tax.
- **Spousal rollover** (on by default): the plan that ends first is treated as the first death. Savings pass to the survivor tax-free and the survivor covers 75% of household spending (you can change it from 40% to 100%).

> **Not modelled**
> CPP and employer-pension survivor benefits are not included. Treat the survivor years as cautious.

# Ontario and Canadian tax rules used
eyebrow: TAX
The planner uses 2026 federal and Ontario rules. These are the main values built in, rounded for reading.

| Ontario brackets | Rate |
| Up to $53,891 | 5.05% |
| $53,891 to $107,785 | 9.15% |
| $107,785 to $150,000 | 11.16% |
| $150,000 to $220,000 | 12.16% |
| Over $220,000 | 13.16% |

| Other Ontario items | 2026 |
| Basic personal amount | $12,989 |
| Age amount (65+) | $6,342, reduced by 15% of net income over $47,210 |
| Pension income amount | up to $1,796 |
| Surtax | 20% of Ontario tax over $5,818, plus 36% of Ontario tax over $7,446 |
| Health Premium | $0 up to $20,000 of income, rising to $750 at $72,600 and $900 at $200,600 |

| Federal items | 2026 |
| Brackets | 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above. No Quebec abatement outside Quebec. |
| Basic personal amount | $16,452 (reduced to $14,829 at high income) |
| Age amount / pension amount | $9,208 (reduced above $46,432) / $2,000 |
| OAS clawback | 15% of net income above $95,323 |

## Other rules
- RRIF / LIF minimum: 1 / (90 minus age) before 71; table from 71 (5.28% at 71, 20% at 95 and over).
- Withholding on registered withdrawals above the minimum: 10% up to $5,000, 20% to $15,000, 30% above. There is no Ontario provincial withholding.
- Ontario LIF maximum: the planner uses the 6% Ontario table (a cautious choice). Ontario also allows the previous year's investment return as an alternative, which is not used.
- Long-run inflation assumption: 2%.
- In August 2026 the Ontario government proposed full unlocking of LIFs. It was only a proposal, so the planner does not assume it. If it becomes law, enter a higher yearly cap or use the unlock option to see the effect.

> **Keeping the rules fresh**
> Tax numbers change every January. In **Menu, Tax & benefit rules**, tap **Copy the prompt**, paste it into any AI chat, paste the code block it returns into the box and tap **Import pasted rates**. The verified date updates by itself. You can also check any figure on the official sites listed at the end of this guide.

# Questions and answers
eyebrow: HELP
## Is my information private?
The planner screens run in your browser and never send your figures anywhere. Cloud scenarios are encrypted on your device with your passphrase before they are stored. The optional AI helper only works when you copy and paste text yourself.

## Will my saved scenarios disappear?
Scenarios saved on this device stay in this browser: clearing the browser's data or using a private window removes them. Cloud scenarios are available on any device with your sign-in and passphrase.

## The plan says my money runs out. What now?
Try, one at a time: spend a little less, retire a year or two later, delay CPP or OAS, or add more each year. After each change, go back to the results (Edit inputs, then Show my plan) and compare.

## Why is "Left after growth" higher than my balance minus the withdrawal?
Your remaining money earns a return during the year. Open "Left after growth" to see the arithmetic.

## What is the difference between today's and future dollars?
Future dollars are the actual cheque amounts; today's dollars remove inflation so numbers feel familiar.

## I do not know my CPP or OAS amount.
Use My Service Canada Account. In the meantime the defaults are reasonable estimates.

## Do I have to follow the plan exactly?
No. It shows how tax and timing interact. Talk to a licensed professional before big decisions.

## My phone shows an old version.
Close the page or Home Screen icon fully and reload it in the browser.

## Reading your results

At the top of the results, **Your plan in a few lines** says in plain words when your money starts, how long it lasts and what your yearly income is, in today's dollars. Open **What if? Test your plan** to try changes, and **Technical details and assumptions** for the detail behind the numbers. On Step 2, each account has a one-line explanation; amounts marked "example" are placeholders to replace with your own. On Step 3, the Cautious, Balanced and Optimistic buttons fill in example returns that you can change.

> **Reading the year plan: spending, taxes, moves.** Under *Spending*, each account line is the money you actually receive. Under *Taxes*, each account line is what the bank withholds from it, and any tax not withheld is shown as money to set aside. If the bank withholds more than the year's tax, a note says the difference comes back when you file. Each year card shows the calendar year above your age.

> **Withheld or set aside?** A tax line marked *(withheld)* is taken by the bank before you ever see the money: it is already paid to the government and you cannot spend it. A line marked *(set aside, do not spend)* is money you do receive, but it is needed for your tax bill, so it must not go to living costs or events.
